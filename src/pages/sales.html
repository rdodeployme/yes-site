01 · The pitch

One sentence, then thirty seconds.

Learn the sentence word for word. The thirty seconds can be in your own words, as long as the three points stay in.

Yarta takes the environmental records an organisation already holds, does the data entry and checking for them, and gives them one score, a live dashboard and a roadmap of what would lift it.

Thirty seconds

“Most councils and businesses have their energy bills, fuel cards, water bills and waste reports sitting in different places, and someone spends days each year stitching them together. Yarta takes those records as they are. Our analysts enter every figure, attach the document it came from, and a second analyst checks the month. You get one score out of 100 across ten categories, measured against the 2030 eco standards, with your emissions by scope and a roadmap that says what would lift your score and by roughly how much. You never key a number in. It’s an annual subscription, and Foundation Members who join in the first 12 months pay no set-up fee.”

01We do the data entry.

They send documents they already hold. A named Yarta data operator enters them and a second analyst verifies the month. This is the biggest difference from software they run themselves.

02Every figure can be traced.

Each number points to a document and an evidence grade. The method, the emission factors and the benchmarks are published. That is what holds up in a council meeting or with an auditor.

03It says what to do next.

The roadmap ranks the improvements by what would move the score, with an estimated range, when it would show up, and accredited providers they can book. Any provider they choose still counts.

03 · Is it a fit

Qualify honestly. A wrong-fit customer costs more than a lost sale.

Our own comparison page tells prospects when something else is the better fit. Say the same thing on the phone.

Good fit

  • Records in several places, with no one person or system pulling them together.
  • A target with a date: 2030 emissions, waste diversion, renewable electricity.
  • Someone asks for the numbers: councillors, a board, an auditor, a customer or a tender panel.
  • A small team that doesn’t want to key data in every month.
  • More than one supplier for waste, energy or fuel, so no single portal shows the whole picture.
  • Wants to know what to do next, not only where they stand.

Not a fit, or not yet

  • They have a data team and only need an emissions inventory. Their own carbon software may be enough.
  • They need financial-statement assurance. We don’t provide it. They still need an assurance provider; we make that provider’s job easier.
  • They want a bespoke strategy written for them. That is consulting work.
  • They only need one contractor’s tonnage. The contractor’s portal does that.
  • They want a certificate or a “carbon neutral” badge. We don’t issue either.
  • No one owns the outcome. Without a named contact the monthly documents won’t arrive.

Councils buy by quote, so sell a 12-month term

A council plan up to 50,000 residents is $23,988 a year. In the procurement policies we checked in September 2026, a spend at that level needs one or two written quotes (for example, Hobsons Bay needs two quotes from $15,000 to $50,000, and Wyndham needs one quote up to $50,000). A three-year deal is about $72,000, which usually moves into the three-quote band and slows everything down. Lead with a 12-month term.

Ask early: “What does your procurement policy need for a purchase of about $24,000 a year?” Each council’s policy is different, so check theirs.

Twelve questions. Ask them, then listen.

You won’t need all of them. The ones in bold type matter most: if you can’t answer those after the call, the call isn’t finished.

Today

  2. How long does it take to pull a report together, and who does it?Days of someone’s time is the cost Yarta removes.

  2. Do you have a baseline year?If not, onboarding sets one with them.
  3. For a business: are you in a climate reporting group, and when is your first Scope 3 year?If they don’t know, send them the checker on the climate reporting page.
  4. If your figures showed you were behind, what would happen next?This is where the roadmap sells itself.

The decision

  1. Who else needs to say yes, and who signs a spend of this size?Get the name and the role.
  2. How many residents, staff or sites?This sets the plan and the price.
  3. When do you need your first report?Work back from it: onboarding takes four weeks.
  4. What would make this a clear yes for you?Write their words down and use them in the proposal.

The establishment fee is normally $5,000 to $15,000, depending on sites, categories and streams. It covers scoping, the baseline year, targets, the method sheet and the four weeks of onboarding.

Customers who join in the first 12 months pay none of it, ever. Their monthly fee is held for the first term, they are consulted before a method change is published, and they can choose to be named as a founding customer. This is your reason to sign now.

What’s in, what isn’t

Included: the dashboard, every figure entered and evidence-graded by Yarta, the monthly gap email, a report per period (and per site for business), CSV and PDF exports, the carbon table by scope, benchmarks, the roadmap updated each quarter, a named data operator and onboarding.

Not included: the work on the roadmap (solar, a fleet change, an audit) which is a separate contract with the provider; offsets or credits; and assurance.

  1. Never discount the monthly fee yourself. The Foundation saving is the offer. Anything else goes to Ryan.
  2. Councils over 50,000 residents are priced on application. Get their population, sites and services, then get the price from Ryan before you put a number in writing.
  3. The price doesn’t change when they add categories or sites. Starting with fewer categories doesn’t make it cheaper either.
  4. Business price is set by total staff, across every site.
  5. Compare against what they spend today: consultant fees, or the staff days spent pulling reports together. The pricing page shows indicative market prices; call them indicative.
  6. Don’t quote the written improvement plans as an extra. Whether they sit inside the subscription or are a paid add-on hasn’t been decided yet.

08 · Objections

The hard questions, and the honest answers.

Agree with what is true in the objection first, then answer it. Never claim more than the product does: an overclaim that wins the sale loses the customer at the first audit.

“We already report to the state, and our contractor has a portal.”

Keep doing both. The contractor’s portal shows that contractor’s waste. The state return covers kerbside. Neither covers energy, fleet, water, land, nature and community, or shows them against the 2030 standards.

“We use the same records, so there’s nothing extra for you to key in. What you get on top is the whole organisation in one score, and what to do about it.”

“We already have carbon software.”

Ask who keys the data in each month. If they have a data team and only need an emissions inventory, their software may be enough; say so. If a small team is keying in invoices, Yarta does that work and adds the other categories, the benchmarks and the roadmap. Yarta’s exports can feed their software.

“Our data’s a mess.”

“That’s normal, and it’s why we do the entry. Send what exists. Anything with no record is marked ‘not reported’ and left out of the score. We never guess it. The monthly email tells you what’s still missing.”

“Our auditor won’t accept a self-declared score.”

Agree: they shouldn’t. The score is a management summary. What an auditor looks at is the figures behind it, and every one comes with its document, its evidence grade, and the factor, source and edition on the carbon table. Yarta doesn’t provide assurance. It makes the assurance provider’s job faster.

“Isn’t this just Recycle Group selling recycling?”

No. They can use any provider for any improvement, and their score doesn’t depend on who does the work. Yarta takes no fee for introductions. Where a Recycle Group business does work, the report says so. Where their material passes through Recycle Group, the recycling figures come from the recycler’s own weighbridge and processing records, which is the strongest kind of evidence for that line.

“It’s too expensive,” or “there’s no budget this year.”

Compare it with what they spend now: staff days pulling reports together, or a consultant’s fees for one report a year. Then the Foundation saving: no establishment fee, up to $15,000, only for the first 12 months. For councils, a 12-month term usually needs only one or two quotes.

“If budget is the blocker, send us one month of records and look at what you’d get before you decide.”

“Can we start with just a few categories?”

Yes. Most start with energy, fleet and waste and add the rest as records come together. Be clear the price is the same either way: it is set by size, not by category.

“Benchmarking means you share our data.”

“It doesn’t. Your data is never shared with, shown to or pooled with another customer. The benchmarks are published figures from named public sources, printed on the report with the source and the year.”

“How do we know the improvement estimates are real?”

They are estimates, shown as a range with the date the effect should show. They come from published rules applied to their own figures, and each one states its assumption. The next report shows what actually changed. Never promise a score.

“Can we say we’re carbon neutral, or use this in our marketing?”

They can’t claim carbon neutral from a Yarta report: we don’t issue offsets or credits. They can publish the report as issued, as long as they don’t alter it or describe the score as accredited or third-party verified. Avoided emissions from recycling are an estimate, shown separately, never subtracted from their total.

“Who are your customers? Can we speak to one?”

Be straight: Yarta is new, and the Foundation Members are its first customers. That is why the establishment fee is waived and the price is held. Offer the one-month trial on their own data instead of a reference. Never name a company as a customer unless Ryan confirms it in writing.

“What happens if we stop?”

They keep every report issued and can export all their data for 90 days after the subscription ends. After that, reports and evidence are kept for the retention period in the privacy policy, then deleted.

“Where is our data stored?”

Don’t answer from memory, and don’t promise Australian-only hosting. Say you’ll send the current hosting statement in writing, and get it from Ryan. Councils often ask this; take it seriously.

“How long until we’re live?”

The plan is four weeks from signing: scope and agreement in week 1, baseline and targets in week 2, the first month entered and verified in week 3, the dashboard live and a handover call in week 4. Confirm current start dates with Ryan before you commit to one.

09 · Say / never say

The words that keep us out of trouble.

Environmental claims are a regulator priority in Australia, and the law applies between businesses too. These rules protect the customer and us. If you aren’t sure a claim is allowed, don’t make it.

Say

  • “The Yarta Score”: a score out of 100 under our own published method.
  • “Self-declared, under a published, versioned method.”
  • “Evidence-graded”: every figure has its document and a grade.
  • “Avoided emissions: an estimate, reported separately”, never part of their total.
  • “Built to support your assurance provider.”
  • “Published industry benchmarks for your sector.”
  • “An estimated range” for every improvement, with the date it should show.
  • “The first step towards being ready for your auditor.”
  • “A Recycle Group initiative.”

Never say

  • Certified, accredited, rated, approved, audited or verified by a third party, about the score or the report.
  • “Makes you compliant” with climate reporting or any standard.
  • Carbon neutral, offset, net zero or carbon saved as something the report gives them.
  • “Compared with other councils” or “peers”. We never use other customers’ data.
  • “Your score will go up by…” as a promise.
  • The demo figures as real results. Every demo organisation is fictional.
  • Any company’s name as a customer, unless Ryan has confirmed it in writing.
  • Best, leading, first, revolutionary or anything we can’t prove.
  • “Free.” Say “no establishment fee for Foundation Members”.

Some demo screens show a working name, “Yindyamarra”. Say “the Yarta Score”. If a prospect asks about the names, say they are being confirmed with the language custodians, and leave it there.

11 · Handover

What the data operator needs from you on day one.

Onboarding moves only as fast as this list. Send it with the signed agreement, and attach their Readiness Check answers.

  • Organisation details: legal name, type, sector, state, and residents or staff.
  • Sites in scope, with an address and a contact for each.
  • The reporting contact: name, role, email and phone.
  • Who signed, the term, the plan and the monthly fee.
  • Foundation Member: yes or no, and the date they joined.
  • Which categories they want to start with, and which come later.
  • The records they hold, who holds each one and their suppliers.
  • Baseline year if they have one, and any targets they have already committed to.
  • When they need their first report, and who it is for.
  • Anything you promised, in writing. If it isn’t written down, onboarding can’t deliver it.

02

The Yindyamarra Score

One number a board can read. Ten categories an auditor can check.

A score out of 100 for each month, the average of the categories you report. Each category is scored 0 to 100 from its own figures against the eco standard or your target. A category you don't report is left out, not scored zero. Self-declared under the published method, versioned, and never an accredited rating or certification.

80 to 100Leading
65 to 79Strong
50 to 64Developing
Under 50Starting

How a category is scored

  • Energy: 60% renewable share, 40% grid electricity against your baseline.
  • Fleet: 60% electrification (full marks at 50% of the fleet), 40% fuel against your baseline.
  • Carbon: rolling 12-month emissions against the same months of the baseline year, towards your reduction target.
  • Water: 50% alternative water share (full marks at 50%), 50% potable use against your baseline.
  • Waste: 70% landfill diversion, 30% contamination.

 

  • Circularity: 50% recycled-content procurement share (full marks at 30%), 30% reuse, 20% stewardship programs.
  • Land: hectares rehabilitated against your annual target.
  • Nature: 50% trees planted, 50% hectares restored, each against your target.
  • Community: participants against your annual target.
  • Governance: 50% data completeness, 30% evidence share, 20% complaints closed.

Beside the score sit two more numbers: the score at the eco standards, and an estimate with the suggested help done. Both are calculated with the same function as the score itself, so they can never disagree with it. The rules and weights are on the method page and the standards on the eco standards page.

05

Exports and integrations

Built to leave the dashboard.

The figures are yours. Every export carries the evidence reference and the method citation, so the number in your annual report is the number on the ledger.

  • CSV of every ledger entry. Date, stream, quantity, bucket, evidence grade, evidence reference and the operator's initials.
  • PDF report. The Yarta Report for the period, print-ready.
  • Scope 3 export. Waste generated in operations, mapped to GHG Protocol Scope 3 Category 5: mass, treatment, factor, NGA edition and method for every line. Avoided emissions travel in a separate table.
  • Annual report figures. Tonnes by fate, steel, landfill avoided and both emissions figures as a citable table, each with its Yarta Method reference and the wording to use.
  • API access. Read access to your ledger and tiles for your own systems. Roadmap

Evidence-based, not marketing-based. No trees saved. Every equivalence shown with its source.

The Category 5 figure uses the government's NGA landfill factors with supplier-specific processing data. Avoided emissions use NSW DECCW 2010 factors, flagged for refresh, and are reported separately: never deducted, never an offset. Streams with no avoided-emissions factor are reported as recovered tonnes with no estimate, and are marked as pending on the report.

06

The difference

A tonnage line is not a report.

Most organisations already receive a number from their contractor. The question is what it can support.

A contractor's tonnage report

  • One figure per invoice, entered by the contractor
  • A diversion percentage with no method behind it
  • No evidence reference a reader can follow
  • No carbon figure, or one without a source
  • A different format from every contractor
  • Nothing that survives an auditor's question

The Yarta Report

  • Every handover entered and verified by Yarta
  • Three buckets, and Stored never counts as recycled
  • An evidence reference and operator's initials on every entry
  • An inventory figure and an avoided figure, never netted, each with its source
  • One method across every stream and every contractor
  • A report ID and a method version that can be reproduced

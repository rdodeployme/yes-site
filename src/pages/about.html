01

Why Yarta exists

The material was always measured. The customer never saw the number.

Councils and businesses hand over problem streams. Recycle Group recycles them. Yarta turns that into figures a board, a councillor or an auditor can rely on.

For years the group has weighed, sorted and processed what councils and businesses hand over: mattresses, steel, timber, tyres, white goods, e-waste, the lot. Every load crossed a weighbridge and every stream had a destination. The customer received an invoice and, at best, a diversion percentage.

Yarta is the reporting system built on top of that record. It takes what actually happened to the material and states it as measured tonnes by fate, recovered steel and landfill volume avoided, plus two emissions figures kept apart: the Category 5 figure for the customer's inventory, and a modelled estimate of the emissions recycling avoided. Each with the method it was calculated with.

Recycling should be more than a collection service or a percentage. It should be a measurable outcome.

Layer 01

Yarta Report

A downloadable report for each project or reporting period: period, streams, tonnes by fate, steel recovered, the Category 5 figure, avoided emissions reported separately, landfill avoided, method version, issuer and report ID.

Layer 02

Yarta Dashboard

A live portal that moves every time a stream is handed over, across the whole organisation, not only what passes through Recycle Group.

The principle

Your data, one method.

You send the records you already hold. Yarta enters the figures, grades the evidence and runs one documented method, so every number can be traced to the record it came from. A monthly email asks the customer to fill in the gaps; Yarta files the answers into Landfilled, Recycled or Stored.

04

Principles

Five rules every Yarta number is held to.

They are the reason a Yarta figure can go into an ESG disclosure, a Scope 3 inventory or a tender without a footnote apologising for it.

  1. Evidence over marketing.

    No "trees saved" headlines. Equivalences appear only with their source, and a figure with no source is not a figure.

  2. One method, versioned.

    Every calculation follows the Yarta Method. Each report states the version it was calculated with, so a number from last year can be reproduced.

  3. Conservative by default.

    Where two published factors exist, Yarta uses the more conservative one: the lower for avoided emissions, the higher for the inventory. Where no factor exists, no avoided emissions are claimed, and they are never netted against the inventory.

  4. Whole-of-organisation.

    All of a customer's recycling is reported, not only what passes through Recycle Group, and each entry says which it is.

  5. Transparent limits.

    Assumptions are labelled as assumptions. Pending categories are shown as pending. Landfilled material counts against the customer, in full.

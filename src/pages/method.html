01

Principles

Six rules every Yarta figure obeys.

If a number on a report cannot be explained by one of these, it does not go on the report.

  1. Measured mass first

    Tonnes from a weighbridge or a processor record. Counts are converted with a stated unit weight, and the unit weight carries its own status.

  2. Recycled, Stored, Landfilled

    Every tonne lands in one of three buckets. Recycled means baled or sorted material delivered to a licensed reprocessor, with a docket, and counts for you. Landfilled counts against you. Stored is neutral until it moves, and is never counted as recycled.

  3. Steel is measured, not assumed

    Steel recovered is the mass that leaves as scrap, credited only on the recycled portion of a load. The 99% mattress figure is Recycle Group's stated plant yield; a report prints the measured yield with its site and study date. Until measured figures replace them, the fractions in the library are marked group or assumption.

  4. Two figures, never netted

    Your Scope 3 Category 5 inventory figure and the avoided emissions are calculated on different bases and reported side by side. Avoided emissions are never deducted from the inventory, never called an offset, and never used for a carbon-neutral claim.

  5. Conservative, cited and versioned

    Every factor carries its source, its vintage and the library version it entered under. Where two credible figures exist, the claim uses the lower: the steel factor is the LCA net figure, not the gross, and an unaudited residual takes the higher mixed-waste landfill factor.

  6. Pending means no claim

    A stream with no avoided-emissions factor is reported as recovered tonnes with no estimate. It is marked pending, never guessed.

02

Two figures, never netted

Your inventory figure. The avoided figure. Kept apart.

The GHG Protocol's Category 5 guidance keeps avoided emissions out of the Scope 3 inventory: they may be reported, but separately, and never deducted. Every Yarta report carries both figures side by side, and a third, the landfill comparison, only as context.

A

Scope 3 Category 5 inventory

What the waste you hand over emits: the processor's energy to receive, sort, shred and bale it (supplier-specific), transport to the processor where it is done for you, and anything landfilled at the government's NGA landfill factor for its waste type. Making new product from the recovered material is the buyer's emissions, not yours.

Goes in your inventory · GHG Protocol waste-type-specific method

B

Avoided emissions

An estimate of the emissions avoided because recovered material displaces virgin production, from published life-cycle factors. Reported beside A with its source, vintage and boundary. Not deducted from A, not an offset and not a carbon-neutral claim.

Reported separately · NSW DECCW 2010 factors, flagged 2010

C

Landfill comparison

What A would have been had every tonne gone to landfill, at the same NGA factors. It shows what diversion did to your inventory. It is context only: never an inventory figure, never a saving to claim.

05

How a figure is built

From a docket to a report, in eight steps.

The same sequence runs for every handover. The engine does the arithmetic. Nobody types a result.

  1. Mass in

    Every handover is recorded as mass. Weighed streams arrive as tonnes from the docket. Counted streams (mattresses, tyres, white goods, vehicles) are converted with the unit weight in the library, which carries its own status.

  2. Pathway split

    The mass is split across Recycled, Stored and Landfilled. The category's default pathway applies unless the handover's actual pathway is recorded, which it is whenever the docket says so.

  3. Steel credited only on the recycled portion

    For steel-bearing streams, steel recovered is mass × steel fraction × steel recovery, capped at the recycled tonnes. A landfilled or stored load claims no steel.

  4. A · Category 5 inventory

    Processing (tonnes handled × the processor's factor) plus transport (tonnes × km × the vehicle factor, where it is done for the customer) plus landfilled tonnes × the NGA landfill factor for the waste type. Stored material carries processing to date only. Material the customer evidences elsewhere carries no processing or transport from Yarta; anything of it landfilled still counts.

  5. B · Avoided emissions, separately

    Recovered steel × 0.44, plus non-steel recycled tonnes × the category factor. A category with no factor carries no estimate and is marked pending. B is printed beside A and is never subtracted from it.

  6. Landfill volume avoided

    What would have gone in (mass × m³ per tonne) less what did. Mattress residual is counted at 25% of its original volume because it is shredded first.

  7. Recovery rate

    Recycled tonnes ÷ tonnes handed over. Stored tonnes are not recycled, so they do not lift the rate until they move. Landfilled tonnes pull it down.

  8. Yarta Score

    Recovery rate, carbon intensity and evidence share combine into one number out of 100 with the published weights below. Computed by the engine, never typed.

06

The Yarta Score

One number. Three parts. Published weights.

A score out of 100 per customer per period, always shown with the tiles beneath it. Proposed at v0.1, published in full, and self-declared: not an accredited rating, certification or third-party verification.

07

Targets, potential and help

Where you are, where the targets are, and what would close the gap.

Beside the Yarta Score, every dashboard and report shows the score at the 2030 targets and an estimate with the recommended help done. The help comes from published rules, so the same figures always give the same recommendations, and any provider can do the work.

Now

—

The Yarta Score for the period, from the handovers Yarta has entered and verified.

At the 2030 targets

—

The same period with each target met: an 80% resource recovery rate (National Waste Policy Action Plan, 2024) and every tonne on Grade A evidence. Carbon performance stays as it is, because there is no target for it.

With Yarta help · estimate

—

The same period with the assumed effect of every recommended item applied in full, recalculated by the same engine as the score. An estimate, not a promise or a guarantee.

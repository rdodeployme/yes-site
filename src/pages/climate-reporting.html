Mandatory climate reporting

Your waste number now has to hold up to an auditor.

Australia's mandatory climate reporting is live. From their second reporting year, companies report their Scope 3 emissions, and an auditor reviews them. Waste is Scope 3, Category 5. Yarta gives you that number from the processor that handled your material, with the evidence behind every line.

01

Who has to report, and when

Three groups. Scope 3 from each group's second year.

A company is in a group if it meets two of the three size tests. Companies that report under the National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting scheme are captured too.

Waste is one of the 15 Scope 3 categories. The standard (AASB S2) requires a company to consider all 15 and disclose the ones it includes, so waste is in the number wherever it is material: property, retail, construction, manufacturing, logistics and hospitality usually; a bank or a software company usually not.

03

What your auditor will ask

Seven questions about your waste number. Each one answered on the report.

Limited assurance means an auditor reviews how the number was built. These are the questions that review asks of a waste figure, and where Yarta answers them.

What's in the boundary, and does it match yours?

On the report: the program, sites, streams and period, and which entries were handed to the processor versus evidenced by you.

Which method did you use for each line?

Stated per line: the processor's own energy use for handling your material, and published landfill factors by waste type for anything landfilled.

Where did each factor come from, and which edition?

Printed with the figure: the National Greenhouse Accounts edition and the source of every factor, pinned to the method version.

How did counts become tonnes?

Published unit weights with their status, and weighbridge tonnes wherever the load was weighed.

How is stored material treated?

Reported separately and never counted as recycled. It moves in the period it moves, with a line pointing back to the original receipt.

Are avoided emissions kept out of the inventory?

Always. Avoided emissions are an estimate in their own box beside the inventory figure, never deducted from it and never an offset.

Show me the evidence for this line.

Every ledger line carries the docket, count sheet or processor record it came from, and the initials of the Yarta operator who entered it. Customers never type their own figures.

04

Three ways to calculate waste emissions

Start at the top: the processor's own data.

The GHG Protocol ranks three methods for waste. Most companies are stuck on the bottom one because nobody can tell them what happened to their material. Yarta works from the top two.

1

Supplier-specific

The waste company's own emissions for handling your material, allocated to your tonnes. Only the processor holds this data.

Yarta · processor data
2

Waste-type-specific

Tonnes by waste type and treatment, multiplied by published factors. Yarta applies the National Greenhouse Accounts landfill factor to every landfilled tonne.

Yarta · every landfilled tonne
3

Average-data

Industry averages or spend-based estimates. Permitted, but it is what a company falls back on when nobody can give it the tonnes.

The fallback

Working processing values are published as illustrative on the Yarta Method page until metered data replaces them. Designed to support Scope 3 Category 5 reporting and third-party assurance; Yarta does not certify compliance with any standard.

05

What's changing

The rules are settling. Scope 3 is staying.

We keep this page current. The position below is as at 28 September 2026.

Treasury consultation · closes 2 October 2026

Easier assurance, fewer supply-chain data requests

Treasury is consulting on reducing compliance costs: changing the assurance rules, clarifying key terms, and reducing the burden of information requests along supply chains. KPMG's reading is that it will not change what is reported for 2026–27, and Scope 3 disclosure is still required.

What that means for waste: a supplier who hands you audit-ready numbers without being asked removes exactly the burden the reform is aimed at.

Councils

Not covered by this law, still asked the question

Councils are not companies under the Corporations Act, so this regime does not apply to them. Their waste reporting runs through state frameworks, such as audited kerbside diversion indicators, and through the companies they contract with, who increasingly need the numbers.

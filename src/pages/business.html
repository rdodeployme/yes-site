02

Scope 3, Category 5

Waste generated in operations is Category 5. Yarta is built for it.

Under the GHG Protocol Scope 3 Standard, the waste your operations generate is reported as Category 5. Yarta records what an assurance provider expects to see behind that line.

What is recorded

Every handover is recorded as mass by stream, with the pathway it took: Recycled, Stored or Landfilled. Each entry carries an evidence reference, a weighbridge docket, processor certificate, count sheet or contractor report, and the grade that evidence earns.

The Category 5 figure follows the GHG Protocol's waste-type-specific method: the processor's own energy to sort, shred and bale your material, transport where it is done for you, and landfilled tonnes at the government's NGA landfill factor for the waste type. The factor edition is pinned to every line, so earlier figures stay reproducible after the library moves.

Avoided emissions are calculated too, from published life-cycle factors, and reported beside the inventory figure, never deducted from it. That is the separation the GHG Protocol asks for, and the one an assurance provider will check first.

The Scope 3 export is the table an assurance provider expects: mass, treatment, factor, source and version, line by line, with the evidence reference beside each.

Multi-site

Sites, regions and the portfolio, from one ledger.

Every entry is tagged to a site. Roll it up by region or read the whole portfolio, with the method held constant across all of it.

A national operator does not recycle in one place. A distribution centre hands over pallets and cardboard, a head office refreshes its IT, a depot clears scrap. Yarta holds each of them as a site with its own ledger, and reads them together as a portfolio.

The monthly fill-in-the-gaps email goes to each site, so the person who knows where the material went is the one who answers.

  • Per site. Its own tiles, ledger, monthly gap email and Yarta Report per period.
  • Per region. Sites grouped the way your organisation is run, with the same tiles at the regional level.
  • Portfolio roll-up. One Yarta Score and one set of tiles for the whole organisation, built from the site ledgers.
  • Yarta Benchmark. Compares sites against each other and each site against its previous period, and shows which streams moved the number.

06

Questions business asks

What you will want to know first.

The questions sustainability, procurement and finance teams ask first.

Can our own recycling contractors' data be included?

Yes. Yarta reports all of your recycling, not only what passes through Recycle Group. Material handled by your existing contractors is entered as whole-of-organisation entries and graded by the evidence behind it. A processor certificate or weighbridge docket counts in full. A contractor report counts and is flagged. A statement with no documentation is recorded and does not count until the evidence arrives.

Who enters the data?

You send the records; Yarta enters them. Your named Yarta data operator enters every figure against the report, certificate, invoice or contractor record you supply, and grades its evidence. That is what lets the figures hold up in front of an assurance provider.

What do we receive each month?

The fill-in-the-gaps email for each site, listing every stream we know moved and every one we cannot yet account for. Once your answers are entered, the dashboard is current and the month's figures are ready to export. A Yarta Report issues at the end of each reporting period.

Can we get the underlying data?

Yes. Yarta Data exports as CSV: every ledger entry with its date, site, stream, quantity, bucket, evidence grade, evidence reference and the operator's initials. The Scope 3 export adds the factor, source and method version for each line.

Is this an offset?

No. Yarta reports two figures: your Category 5 inventory figure, and the emissions recycling avoided, reported separately under a stated method. It does not issue offsets or credits. The avoided figure is never deducted from your inventory and should not be presented as an offset, a credit or a carbon-neutral claim.

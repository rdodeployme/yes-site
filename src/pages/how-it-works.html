01

The loop

Six steps. One of them is yours.

You send the data. Yarta does the entering, the grading and the calculating. The loop runs every period, and closes when the report issues with its roadmap.

  1. Onboard

    Thirty days. Yarta agrees your categories, your sites, your targets and your baseline year with you, and issues the method sheet that states how each one will be counted. Nothing is calculated until the method sheet is agreed.

  2. Send your data

    Waste and recycling reports, utility bills, fuel and fleet records, invoices, contractor reports. Whatever you already hold for the period, for the whole organisation or one program.

  3. Yarta enters and grades

    Yarta enters the figures from your records. Every entry carries an evidence reference and a grade, so any figure on your dashboard can be traced back to the document it came from.

  4. Your dashboard moves

    As the data arrives. Every tile recalculates: the ten category scores, the Yindyamarra Score, where you could be at the eco standards, and the emissions figures: your inventory by scope and, beside it, the avoided emissions.

  5. Fill in the gaps

    Once a month an automated email lists what Yarta still needs for the period, by category. You send it, or say it does not exist. A category with no data is marked "not reported" and left out of the score, never guessed.

  6. Report issues, with the roadmap

    Monthly, quarterly or annual, to suit your reporting cycle. The report states every figure with the method version it was calculated under, pinned so the number can be reproduced later, and closes with the roadmap to improvement: the score at the eco standards, the suggestions, the estimate with Yarta help and the plan by quarter.

02

The roadmap to improvement

From your data to a better score. Five steps, every period.

A report that only measures leaves you where you were. Yarta evaluates, benchmarks, suggests, introduces the accredited help, and measures the change on the next report.

Evaluate

Your data becomes ten category scores and one Yindyamarra Score, each with the rule that produced it. The weakest categories are the ones the roadmap starts with.

Yarta

Benchmark and compare

Each category is set against the eco standards, the published industry benchmark for your sector and your own history. The gap between where you are and "at the eco standards" is the size of the opportunity.

Yarta

Suggest

The roadmap lists the suggestions that would move the score most: switch to renewable electricity, add an organics service, fix the contamination, electrify the next fleet purchase. Each one carries its estimated effect on the score.

Yarta

Book accredited help

Every suggestion can be booked. Yarta introduces a provider who holds the licence or accreditation the work requires, or you use your own. The booking is recorded against the roadmap.

You choose · Yarta introduces

Measure the change

The next report shows the category that moved, the figure behind it and who did the work. The roadmap rolls forward with the next set of suggestions.

03

Whole-of-organisation

Every category. Whoever supplies it.

Yarta reports the whole organisation: the electricity retailer's bill, the fuel-card statement, the water meter, every waste contractor, the planting record and the community program. Each figure is graded by the evidence behind it, wherever it came from.

1 Email a month is your part

It lists what Yarta still needs for the period, by category. You forward the bill, the statement or the contractor report, or say it does not exist. None of your suppliers ever touch Yarta, and none need to.

The rule is the same for every category: a Yarta operator enters it, the entry carries an evidence reference, and the evidence decides how much the figure counts. That is what lets one dashboard hold an energy bill, a fleet register, a kerbside contract and a revegetation program at once, and still put a number on the report that an auditor can follow back.

Evidence is graded, not assumed. A bill and a customer's say-so are both recorded. They do not carry the same weight.

What you receive

The dashboard, the report and the method behind both.

Everything on this list is built from one record, so the number on the report is the number on the dashboard is the number in the export.

  • Yarta Dashboard access. Your score, the ten categories, where you could be and the suggestions, live, across every site. Moves the month the data lands.

  • Yarta Report per period. Three pages: the score and the categories, the figures behind them, and the roadmap to improvement, with a report ID and the method version.
  • CSV and PDF exports. Every entry with its evidence reference, the carbon export by scope, and the Yarta Report as a PDF.
  • Roadmap and provider introductions. The suggestions by quarter, the estimate with Yarta help, and an accredited provider for each one you choose to book.
  • A named Yarta data operator. One person who enters your records, answers the gap email and signs off the month with their initials.

The principle

Your data in. Your results out. Then the roadmap.

If customers typed in their own figures, no report built on them would be credible. So they do not. Your part of the loop is to send the records you already hold and answer one email a month. Yarta does the rest, and the roadmap tells you what to do next.

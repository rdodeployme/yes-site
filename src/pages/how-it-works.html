01

The loop

Six steps. One of them is yours.

You hand the material over. YES does the entering, the checking and the calculating. The loop runs every time a stream moves, and closes when the certificate issues.

  1. Onboard

    Thirty days. YES agrees your streams, your sites and your baseline period with you, and issues the method sheet that states how each stream will be counted. Nothing is calculated until the method sheet is agreed.

  2. Hand over a stream

    A hard waste sweep, a skip of scrap steel, a pallet of e-waste. Every handover arrives with its evidence: a weighbridge docket, a count sheet or a processor certificate.

  3. YES enters and verifies

    Only YES staff enter data. Every entry carries an evidence reference and the operator's initials, so any figure on your dashboard can be traced back to the document it came from.

  4. Your dashboard moves

    The day the material moves. Every tile recalculates: tonnes by fate, steel recovered, landfill avoided, the material recovery rate, the YES Score, and the two emissions figures: your Category 5 figure and, beside it, the avoided emissions.

  5. Fill in the gaps

    Once a month an automated email lists every stream we know moved and every one we cannot yet account for. You tell us where it went. YES files it as Landfilled, Recycled or Stored.

  6. Certificate issues

    Monthly, quarterly or annual, to suit your reporting cycle. The certificate states every figure with the method version it was calculated under, pinned so the number can be reproduced later, and says who issued it: Recycle Group, as the processing operator.

There is no fourth bucket and no rounding up. What went to landfill counts against you. What was recycled counts for you. What is waiting counts for nothing until it moves.

Why Stored exists

Some material has to wait. E-waste is batched until there is enough for a processor run. Textiles sit while a textile pathway is confirmed. Coloured and treated timber has no recycling route modelled yet. A report that quietly counted that material as recycled would be wrong, and one that counted it as landfilled would be unfair. So YES parks it, in the open, and moves it the day it moves.

What reclassification looks like

When a stored batch reaches a processor, the YES operator enters the processor certificate, the tonnes leave Stored and land in Recycled, and every tile recalculates. If a pathway is never found and the batch goes to landfill, it moves to Landfilled instead. Either way the ledger shows both entries and both dates.

03

Whole-of-organisation

All of your recycling. Not only ours.

YES reports every stream your organisation recycles, wherever it went. Material that passes through Recycle Group arrives with its own docket. Material handled elsewhere is graded by the evidence behind it.

23,000 Wheelie bins collected this month

A council reports the count and states where the material went. YES enters it, grades the evidence, and the dashboard moves. The council's kerbside contractor never touched YES and did not need to.

The rule is the same for every stream: a YES operator enters it, the entry carries an evidence reference, and the evidence decides how much the figure counts. That is what lets one dashboard hold a hard waste program, a transfer station, a kerbside contract and a depot clearance at once, and still put a number on the certificate that an auditor can follow back.

Evidence is graded, not assumed. A weighbridge docket and a customer's say-so are both recorded. They do not carry the same weight.

05

What you receive

The dashboard, the certificate and the method behind both.

Everything on this list is built from one ledger, so the number on the certificate is the number on the dashboard is the number in the export.

  • YES Dashboard access. Your organisation's recycling, live, across every site and program. Moves the day a stream moves.
  • Monthly gap email and summary. What moved, what is unaccounted for, and where the month landed across the three buckets.
  • YES Certificate per period. One page a board, a councillor or an auditor can read in a minute, with a certificate ID and the method version.
  • CSV and PDF exports. Every ledger entry with its evidence reference, and the certificate and report as PDF.
  • Method pack. The factor library, every unit-weight assumption and the version history, so any figure can be reproduced.
  • A named YES data operator. One person who enters your handovers, answers the gap email and signs off the ledger with their initials.

The principle

Only YES enters the data. That is why the number holds.

If customers typed in their own figures, no report built on them would be credible. So they do not. Your part of the loop is to hand the material over and answer one email a month.

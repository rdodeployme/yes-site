01

The reporting problem

The data sits in six departments. The score doesn't exist.

Waste has the contractor reports, fleet has the fuel cards, facilities has the bills, parks has the planting records. Nobody holds one number for the council, and nobody can say what would move it.

What you get today

  • Tonnage lines on contractor invoices, bills in a drawer, a fleet register nobody reconciles
  • A diversion percentage with no method behind it
  • An environment strategy with targets and no measurement against them
  • Nothing that survives an audit or a council meeting
  • No plan for what to do first

With Yarta

  • One score across ten categories, from the records you already hold
  • Every figure evaluated against the eco standards and your own targets
  • A Yarta Report each period, with the roadmap to improvement
  • A dashboard for the sustainability team, a page for the community
  • Figures cited by method version in the annual report

05

Report-ready

An evidenced number for the annual report, the tender and the councillor.

Every figure on a Yarta Report carries its evidence grade, its eco standard and its method version, so it can be cited in the annual report, a tender response or a contract KPI without re-checking.

  • Annual and tender reporting. The Yindyamarra Score, the ten category scores, tonnes by stream, energy, water and fleet for the period, and the emissions figures, each with its Yarta Method reference.
  • Contract KPIs and benchmarks. Diversion, contamination and evidence share per contractor, calculated the same way for every contractor and set against the eco standards and the industry benchmark for councils of your size.
  • Evidence grades. Grade A, B or C on every entry, so a reader can see what a figure rests on before they rely on it.
  • Method versioning. The version is printed on the report. The figure can be reproduced later even if the factor library has moved on.
  • Exports. CSV of the ledger, the Yarta Report as a PDF, and a citable table for the annual report.

One method across every department and every contractor. That is what makes the numbers comparable, year on year and against the eco standards.

The Yindyamarra Score and the evidence grades are self-declared under Yarta's own published method, versioned. They are not an accredited rating, certification or third-party verification. 06

Questions councils ask

Straight answers.

The questions waste teams and finance teams ask first.

Do we enter the data ourselves?

No. You send the records you already hold and Yarta enters the figures. Your named Yarta data operator enters every figure against the contractor report, the energy or water bill, the fuel-card statement, the planting record or the invoice you supply, and the entry carries an evidence reference and a grade. That is what makes the report credible to an auditor, a councillor or a resident.

What if we cannot supply a figure for a category?

The category is marked "not reported" and left out of the score; the score is the mean of the categories you do report. Once a month the fill-in-the-gaps email lists every stream and category we cannot yet account for. You send what you have, we enter it, and the evidence you supply decides its grade. A bill or weighbridge record is grade A; a contractor report is grade B; a stated figure is grade C and is flagged.

Who are we benchmarked against?

The eco standards first: the national targets for waste recovery, emissions and renewable electricity, and your own targets where no national standard exists. Then the published industry benchmark for councils, drawn from public sources such as state waste data and national reports, never from other Yarta customers. Your data is not shared with any other organisation, and no other organisation's data is shown to you.

Is the Yindyamarra Score an accredited rating?

No. The Yindyamarra Score is self-declared under Yarta's own published method, versioned with the Yarta Method and shown in full: the mean of the ten category scores you report, each with its rule and weights stated. It is proposed at v0.1. It is not an accredited rating, certification or third-party verification, and is never presented as one. Where a category needs accredited work, the roadmap points you to the right accredited provider.

How are the emissions figures calculated?

Scope 1 and 2 from your fuel and electricity records, and the Scope 3 categories you report, all at the National Greenhouse Accounts factors for the year. The rolling 12 months is compared with the same months of your baseline year and with your reduction target. Avoided emissions from recycling are shown separately as an estimate and never deducted from the inventory. Every factor, source and version is on the Yarta Method page.

Can we show this to residents?

Yes. The Yarta Report can be published as issued, and the annual report can cite its figures by method version. A community view of the dashboard, so residents can see the council's score and what changed this year, is on the roadmap. Roadmap
